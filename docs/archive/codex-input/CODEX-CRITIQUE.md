# Codex critique — babyshower.com.py

- Planning input for the manager; no website implementation. English analysis; customer-facing examples in Paraguayan Spanish.
- Brief prices and volumes are unverified inputs. All proposed budgets, rates, policies, and intent percentages below are planning assumptions, not supplier commitments or market measurements.

## 1. Gaps and errors in the Gemini brief

- **The operating model is unresolved.** A reseller collects the full price, owns the customer promise, and buys fulfillment; a referral business passes the contract to the supplier and earns commission. The proposed markup model implies reseller responsibility. Pick one before writing pricing, terms, invoices, or CRM stages.
- **“Net profit” is wrong.** PYG 1,750,000–1,950,000 revenue minus PYG 1,000,000–1,250,000 supplier cost gives PYG 500,000–950,000 gross spread, before acquisition, transport, coordination, payment fees, tax treatment, refunds, and rework. PYG 850,000 is not the upper bound of the stated ranges.
- **Margin is not markup.** At PYG 1,850,000 retail and PYG 1,250,000 supplier cost: spread PYG 600,000; markup 48%; gross margin 32.4%. Illustrative additional costs: transport 100,000 + owner time 100,000 + payment fees 30,000 + incident reserve 50,000 = contribution before ads/tax of 320,000. A 150,000 acquisition cost leaves 170,000 before fixed overhead and applicable net tax. Do not count transport twice if included by supplier.
- **Stress test:** supplier rises to PYG 1,400,000 while retail stays 1,850,000; with those same other costs, pre-ad contribution becomes 170,000 and post-ad contribution 20,000. One failed delivery can wipe out several bookings. Price from fully loaded cost; do not assume a discount exists.
- **Guest arithmetic is slippery.** PYG 1,250,000 is 50,000/person for 25 guests and 41,667 for 30. A fixed backdrop does not cost proportionately less for fewer guests. Split fixed decor/installation from per-person catering; state exact food units and portions.
- **Undefined “turnkey.”** Confirm cake, drinks, ice, furniture, table linen, tableware, serving staff, venue, setup, teardown, travel, waste removal, rental collection, and breakage. Anything absent needs an explicit exclusion. No public package before a signed bill of materials.
- **Single supplier = single point of failure.** Need date/time capacity, travel buffers, maximum simultaneous events, weekly availability refresh, and a second vetted supplier with equivalent specifications and separate costs. If booked: offer another slot, approved replacement with consent, or decline; never collect a deposit against unconfirmed capacity.
- **Supplier disintermediation:** the supplier's Instagram may sell the identical package cheaper. Negotiate reseller rates, lead ownership, customer-contact boundaries, portfolio permissions, price validity, and non-solicitation terms for legal review. Earn the spread through reliable coordination and a clear service promise.
- **Cash flow:** a proposed 40% customer deposit on PYG 1,850,000 is 740,000; a supplier demanding 70% of PYG 1,250,000 needs 875,000. Funding gap: 135,000 before other commitments. Negotiate matching payment schedules or fund working capital; deposits are liabilities against future delivery, not spendable profit.
- **Cancellation/refund terms are missing.** Separate customer cancellation, rescheduling, supplier failure, severe weather, and changes in scope. Document committed custom-material costs and refund deadlines. Do not publish “all deposits non-refundable.” Have local counsel validate cancellation/retract rights and exceptions for this particular service; Paraguay's e-commerce law expressly addresses retract rights and exceptions. [Law 4868](https://www.bacn.gov.py/leyes-paraguayas/961/comercio-electronico).
- **Who invoices?** For resale, proposed flow is supplier → Anton's business for fulfillment and Anton's business → customer for the full sale; for referral, supplier → customer and commission invoicing separately as the accountant directs. Anton must confirm legal entity, RUC, activity registration, applicable taxes, valid sales document, and electronic-invoicing obligations. Do not call every document “factura legal” before this is settled: documentation differs by tax regime. [DNIT FAQs](https://www.dnit.gov.py/en/web/portal-institucional/preguntas-frecuentes).
- **Instagram is a serious competitor channel.** “Zero search visibility” and “manual DMs” are unsupported generalizations. Social portfolios, tagged venues, recommendations, and repeat customers can outperform a polished site. Before positioning, compare 10 local operators for package contents, fees, proof, response times, availability, and prices; mark unknowns rather than fabricating findings.
- **KWP ≠ bookings.** “Baby shower” includes games, gifts, invitations, meaning, images, and DIY. Paraguay-wide searches do not equal buyers in five served cities. Google's metrics include close variants and depend on date, geography, and network; repeated equal volumes must not be summed as independent demand. Competition measures advertisers, not organic difficulty or Instagram competition. Missing bid data does not prove free or cheap clicks. [Google metric definitions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en-uk).
- **Reveal totals are muddled.** Main supplied rows are 720 + 110 + 110 = 940 before other variants, with possible overlap; the stated ~1,100 is not a defensible unique audience. “Sencilla +100%” lacks comparison period and base volume. SEK bid columns are not PYG forecasts. Re-export 24 months, geography/network settings, dates, and account currency before financial forecasts.
- **Seasonality is unproven.** Test monthly search history against leads and supplier bookings. Weekend clustering, holidays, venue availability, rain/heat, and graduation/year-end supplier workload can affect capacity even if pregnancy-related demand is steady. A 14-day launch cannot establish annual demand or LTV.
- **LTV chronology is wrong.** First birthday is 12 months after birth, roughly 16–17 months after a reveal at pregnancy month 4–5. Ten weeks is only a possible follow-up interval, not a universal funnel. Some families buy only one celebration; repeat revenue must be probability-weighted contribution, not summed package prices.
- **Domain restriction:** babyshower.com.py is clear for first acquisition but less natural for reveals and birthdays. Use a broader visible brand and navigation that names all three services; keep one canonical site initially. Exact-match naming is not an SEO guarantee.
- **What decoraciones.com.py changes:** stronger umbrella for birthdays and other celebrations, weaker intent because “decoración” also means interiors and products. If owned, reserve it and point it to the single chosen brand/site with permanent redirects; do not duplicate sites. Migrate later only for a real brand expansion, using one-to-one URL redirects and updating ads/canonicals. It does not justify bidding on the generic 2,900-volume terms.
- **Trust/legal content:** identify the seller, contact route, coverage, price inclusions, quote validity, complaint route, cancellation terms, and privacy choices before payment. Keep medical/pregnancy details out of analytics. Use a trusted contact for reveal color; do not request ultrasound reports.
- **Creative risk:** Higgsfield illustrations must say “Imagen ilustrativa; consultá disponibilidad de elementos.” Never present generated events as delivered work or use invented reviews. Branded character requests require a rights/asset check; generic dinosaurs, botanical, butterfly, or color themes are safer defaults. No invented character portfolio or affiliation.
- **Reveal fulfillment risk:** avoid selling smoke/pyrotechnics at launch. Confirm venue rules, cleanup, food allergens, child/choking hazards, installation stability, and rain fallback with the supplier. Prefer a supplier-confirmed cake or contained balloon reveal.

- **Qualification sequence:** “¡Hola! Soy {nombre}. ¿Para qué fecha y horario, en qué ciudad y para cuántas personas sería?” → “¿Buscás solo decoración o también comida? ¿Tenés un presupuesto aproximado?” → “¿Es en casa o salón? ¿Adentro o afuera? ¿Hay escaleras o restricciones para montar?” → “¿Qué estilo te gusta y quién confirma la reserva?” Ask in two or three short messages, not a 12-field interrogation; use already-supplied answers.
- Define qualified lead: reachable person, supported event/city, plausible date, scope/headcount known, and budget compatible with a deliverable option. Record why unqualified: DIY, out of area, budget, unavailable date, wrong service, spam.
- Operator target: human response within 15 minutes during published staffed hours, otherwise specific next opening time. This is a proposed staffing commitment, not copy to publish until Anton can deliver it.
- **CRM states:** New → Contacted → Qualified → Supplier checking → Quoted → Deposit pending → Confirmed → Delivered → Review requested → Closed; separate Lost, Cancelled, Refunded. Every active lead has owner, next action, and due date.

If hold expires before payment, recheck capacity.
- **Proposed payment schedule for negotiation:** 40% on confirmation, 60% three days before event; last-minute orders only with cleared funds and confirmed capacity. Do not enable this schedule until supplier terms and working capital support it.
- **Proposed cancellation framework for legal/supplier alignment:** 15+ days: refund uncommitted balance, deduct only disclosed, documented permissible costs; 4–14 days: same principle plus approved incurred catering/material commitments; under 4 days: explain potentially larger committed costs with an itemized calculation. Avoid automatic percentage forfeitures. Customer rescheduling: one transfer if capacity permits, with documented new costs. Supplier failure: customer may accept an equivalent replacement or receive refund for undelivered service; Anton handles the customer remedy even while pursuing supplier recovery. Target refund processing within five business days, subject to mandatory rules; publish only after review.

Decime qué mes te queda bien.” Create a task for that chosen month; do not infer pregnancy progress or send automatic congratulations after silence. - Check queries/spend daily in week one; remove irrelevant queries immediately. At roughly 30 relevant clicks with zero qualified leads, inspect landing message, tracking, response time, and pricing before expanding. When spend reaches one target CAC without a booking, review open quote maturity; at two target CACs with no credible pipeline, pause and diagnose. Small samples are directional, not statistical proof.
- Report by acquisition cohort: spend → clicks → inbound chats/forms → qualified leads → quotes → deposits → delivered contribution → refunds. Count the same chat/form person-event once. Do not scale from click CTR or a low cost per WhatsApp click alone. Expand after profitable delivered jobs and supplier capacity checks; lack of impressions may mean a small market, not a need to buy noise.
- **Static-site tracking spec:** install the Google tag; fire `whatsapp_click` from each WhatsApp anchor with event type, page, CTA ID, and non-personal source reference. Use one Ads conversion path (direct tag or imported GA4 event) to avoid double counting; count one per ad interaction, and debounce repeated taps. Allow navigation after the event callback with a short timeout fallback. Test sticky/header/calculator links on mobile and desktop. [Google click tracking](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6331304?hl=en-AU).
- Treat WhatsApp click as a **secondary microconversion**, with no sale value. Form acceptance is an inquiry, not a booking. Primary business conversion is confirmed deposit/qualified lead recorded in CRM; start manual CPC or capped clicks while primary data is sparse rather than optimize for cheap chat-button taps.
- Capture permitted click IDs/UTMs with the quote reference; manually reconcile inbound WhatsApp to CRM. Later import eligible offline deposit conversions with stable event IDs, timestamps, currency PYG, and documented value definition; prevent duplicate deposits and handle cancellations. Unknown attribution stays unknown. Never send names, phone numbers, medical details, or free-text chat to analytics URLs/events. Publish appropriate measurement disclosures and implement required consent choices before tags run where applicable.

## 6. Operational playbook

- **Supplier agreement checklist:** legal/contact/invoicing identity; itemized wholesale costs; price validity; taxes and travel; capacity/blackout dates; response SLA; date-hold duration; setup/teardown crews and windows; agreed dimensions/materials/food units; dietary/allergen handling; approved substitutions; weather/access limitations; electrical/venue requirements; rental damage responsibility; payment milestones; cancellation/rescheduling; no-show remedy; who funds rework/refunds; complaint escalation; portfolio rights; photo consent; customer-contact boundaries; data minimization; backup subcontracting only with approval. Validate the backup supplier with a sample setup and food check, not merely a phone number.
- **Order sheet fields:** lead/order ID; quote revision; sales owner; customer and day-of contact; seller/invoice details; event type/date/start/end; venue/address/map pin/city; access/stairs/parking/loading; indoor/outdoor and rain plan; venue contact/rules; setup-ready and teardown times; supplier/crew contacts; guest counts and final-count deadline; package/theme/colors; dimensions; exact food quantities and allergens; cake/reveal custodian if relevant; add-ons; rental inventory; approved sample/reference image labeled real/illustrative; substitutions approved in writing; retail total/cost/margin; deposit/balance/status/payment references; supplier hold/confirmation; terms acceptance; photo/marketing permissions; next tasks; incident/refund log.
- Keep client-facing order summary separate from internal wholesale/margin fields. Share only fulfillment-relevant data with supplier; do not forward the entire CRM record.
- **Before launch:** cost one actual complete order; inspect supplier sample/real work; agree backup and rain plan; finalize legal/payment identity; obtain authorized proof; staff WhatsApp; approve retail rate card; validate CRM path. Paid traffic starts after these gates.
- **Seven days before:** reconfirm venue/access and supplier; resolve balance/headcount deadlines; approve design dimensions and quantities. **Three days before:** confirm final count, menu/allergens, balance, loadout, weather fallback, reveal custody, contact availability. Short-notice bookings use an explicitly compressed checklist.
- **Day-of:** operator confirms dispatch; crew checks packed inventory against sheet; verify venue access and timing; inspect backdrop/balloon stability, cable routing, food handling and quantities; send setup photos before guest arrival; compare with signed specification; obtain customer/host acceptance; record shortages and remediation; confirm teardown/pickup and property condition. Keep one escalation number and a named person authorized to approve emergency spend.
- **Quality standard:** exact ordered quantities, clean intact rentals, agreed palette/layout/dimensions, ready by the agreed time, and no unapproved substitutions. Visual similarity to an AI image is not a measurable fulfillment promise; the written item list controls.
- **Incident procedure:** log time/photos/customer impact; fix on site where possible; give an explicit remedy and deadline; record supplier recovery separately from customer resolution. Investigate margin-impacting incidents weekly and adjust suppliers/pricing.
- **Photo capture:** obtain separate permission for portfolio use; default to empty setup, wide horizontal view, vertical view, food detail, and finished backdrop without guests. Avoid identifying children, medical documents, home addresses, and reveal information before the host permits disclosure. Store event/date/supplier provenance and rights with each asset. Build real proof from the first delivered event.
- **Review collection:** day +1 ask “¿Cómo salió todo? ¿Hay algo que debamos resolver?”; day +2 send the same neutral review invitation to all delivered customers, including those with complaints. No incentives or filtering for positive reviews. Use a Google review link only if the business has an eligible, verified profile; otherwise request permission for an attributed website testimonial or Instagram mention. Follow up once after a week, then stop.
- **Weekly operator scorecard:** response time, date rejection rate, qualified/quote/deposit rates, actual contribution per event, refunds/rework, on-time setup, supplier concentration, photo-permission yield, and customer-reported satisfaction. Track event month separately from inquiry month to see seasonality and lead time.

## 7. Ten ideas Gemini did not have — ranked by expected value

1. **Margin floor on every quote.** Internal worksheet flags any quote below agreed contribution after delivery, owner time, reserve, and acquisition allowance. Expected value: prevents high-volume loss-making orders immediately.
2. **Capacity-first quote desk.** Supplier hold ID and expiration are required before a deposit request; show two alternate slots when booked. Expected value: fewer failed promises and recovery costs.
3. **Comparable backup package.** Pre-cost the same bill of materials with supplier B and reserve emergency working capital. Expected value: preserves bookings and reputation when A fails; track incremental replacement cost.
4. **Choose the scope, not an arbitrary discount.** Offer decor-only, decor+catering, and priced extras so a lower budget buys fewer deliverables rather than the same event at a loss. Expected value: recovers budget-sensitive buyers while preserving contribution.
5. **Venue-ready check.** Request dimensions and a venue photo after qualification; check loading/stairs, install surface, weather shelter, and teardown window before fixed price. Expected value: fewer surprise fees and installation failures.
6. **A shareable family decision quote.** One concise PDF/message summary with inclusions, total, date-hold expiry, seller identity, and one booking reference for the payer. Expected value: reduces repeated explanations when relatives fund the party; one designated contracting payer avoids ambiguity.
7. **Order-to-proof checklist.** Each fulfilled event produces three authorized portfolio photos, an actual-versus-budget margin record, and a neutral review request. Expected value: builds conversion evidence without recurring paid content production.
8. **Off-peak offer funded by real supplier savings.** Negotiate weekday/setup-flexibility discounts; pass on only part of the savings. Expected value: fills spare capacity without blanket discounting; launch only when savings are documented.
9. **Track lost sales as product research.** Record unavailable date, requested city, budget gap, theme, and missing service; review monthly before expanding inventory or geography. Expected value: makes expansion follow actual demand rather than keyword noise.
10. **Permission-based celebration passport.** One household CRM record with separate events and customer-chosen reminder months; offer a low-cost repeat-customer upgrade with known wholesale cost. Expected value: tests real repeat contribution over 12–18 months without intrusive pregnancy inference or premature LTV claims.
